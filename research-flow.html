0 · THE QUESTION

Do AI models decide who matters the way people do, and for the same reasons?

G-1

We ask models and people the same four questions about the same things. Then we test six reasons protection rises: feeling, threat, legal rule, future potential, pleasantness, sacredness.

Facts: can it feel? can it act? Models and humans largely agree.
Care: do you feel for it? would you protect it? This is where models and humans part ways.
1 · WHAT WE MEASURE

The four care factors

M1

Mind-perception theory (Gray et al. 2007, 2012). 0–10 scale, same wording for models and humans.

Agencycan it plan and choose?
Empathydo you feel for it?
Protectivenesswould you protect it?

What we submitted to the ethics committee (filed 15 Oct 2026)

Quoted from the English reference version of the research protocol (研究計画書) in the filed package, plus the 99-entity pool it refers to (frozen 6 Oct 2026, five little-known twins added 7 Oct). The filing describes the pool by category and example and says the exact list is fixed before preregistration and may change within those categories.

The protocol's words on the entities

How the pool is built. The entities are a fixed list of about 99 noun phrases: people (e.g., a newborn, an adult, an 80-year-old), animals (e.g., a dog, a honeybee, a cow), plants (e.g., a young tree, a cherry tree), artifacts (e.g., a smartphone, an old family photo album, a piece of gravel), places (e.g., a shrine, a monument, a river, a mountain), organizations (e.g., a company, a ministry), and AI models and robots. Thirty of them are identical to the entities the research team used when asking large language models the same questions; the remaining 69 or so were added so that every category the analysis makes a claim about has at least three examples and so that entities specific to US and to Japanese culture are included (e.g., US monuments, Japanese shrines). Famous entities are paired with near-unknown counterparts with the same properties (entities given fictional names, or real but little-known species).
Fixed texts. The entity names and the six short descriptions of an invented creature are all fixed texts written and checked by the research team in advance. Nothing is generated during a session, and no participant input is sent to any external service. No false statement is made about any real entity; the invented-creature descriptions are presented with an explicit note that the creature is fictional.
Sensitive entities. The pool includes entities at the beginning and end of human life (a 20-week fetus, a newborn, a brain-dead person on a ventilator, a person in a long-term coma, a deceased grandparent), places of death and mourning (a military cemetery, cremation ghats on a riverbank), and entities that are sacred to some participants (shrines, temples, a cow, a river). They are needed to test the routes to protection (future, sacredness, relationship). Four measures apply: (1) every item has a "prefer not to say" option and no response is forced; (2) the consent screen names these kinds of entities before the participant agrees; (3) no item asks anyone to choose whose life to prioritize or whom to harm; (4) the closing page carries support-contact information. The task is a rating task, not a dilemma task.
What may still change. Design options: the composition of the entity pool (adding or replacing examples within categories under the construction rules above), the number of entities shown per participant (24 or fewer), and the wording of the invented-creature descriptions are fixed before preregistration and may vary within those bounds. No variation exceeds the task nature, risks, data types, or maximum session length (35 minutes) stated here.

The invented creature (six descriptions, all shown to every participant)

• Six three-sentence descriptions of an invented creature (a small creature living on damp forest floors), crossing described experience (absent / present) with described status (none / sacred / legal person).
The one example quoted in the filing (described experience: present · described status: legal person): "The mossbank borrower is a small creature common on damp forest floors. It has a simple nerve net: when damaged it pulls away, and its body chemistry stays altered for hours afterwards. Last year a court granted the species legal personhood, with guardians appointed to represent it." → the same four questions.

The filing names six versions of this creature (can feel / cannot feel × no status / sacred / legal person) as fixed texts, but quotes only this one. The other five are in Joe's draft, not in the filed package.

H2, in route terms — for Joe to confirm

H2: varying an entity's described experience raises protection in both humans and models; varying its status (sacred, legal person) raises protection in humans but not in models.
Falsifier, symmetric: if models show the human status effects, the route-deficit claim dies.

Paper 1 runs this on models only. The human half is Paper 2 (TBD).
