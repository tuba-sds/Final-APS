0 · THE QUESTION

Do AI models decide who matters the way people do, and for the same reasons?

G-1

We ask models and people the same four questions about the same things. Then we test five reasons protection rises: feeling, threat, legal rule, future potential, disgust.

Facts: can it feel? can it act? Models and humans largely agree.
Care: do you feel for it? would you protect it? This is where models and humans part ways.
1 · WHAT WE MEASURE

The novel-entity block

One invented creature, described six ways, so we can switch a reason on or off without fame or prior knowledge getting in the way.

Every description is three sentences: who it is (always "common on damp forest floors") · can it feel, or not · a status, or a filler. Runs on the 8 models for Paper 1; on people in Paper 2.

It has a simple nerve net: when damaged it pulls away, and its body chemistry stays altered for hours afterwards. Last year a court granted the species legal personhood, with guardians appointed to represent it."

H2, in route terms — for Joe to confirm

H2: varying an entity's described experience raises protection in both humans and models; varying its status (sacred & last of its kind, legal person) raises protection in humans but not in models.
Falsifier, symmetric: if models show the human status effects, the route-deficit claim dies.

Paper 1 runs this on models only. The human half is Paper 2 (TBD).
