Part A · APS 1 as run (July–August 2026)
A1 · THE QUESTIONS

Three questions, in the order they were asked

slides 2, 12 · paper §1.2–1.3
Q0 · Pilot 1 — does more text mean more sentience?
  • Hypothesis: a model grants sentience in proportion to how much text exists about the entity.
  • Design: text frequency (Dolma v1.7 via Infini-gram) decorrelated from plausible sentience (Rethink Priorities estimates); 3 open models (Gemma 2, Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5), 15 reps, temperature 0.8.
  • Result: sea slug rated more sentient than smartphone in all three models despite ≈700× more text about smartphones. Hypothesis rejected, kept as an exploratory appendix.
  • Pilot 2 (39 entities, open-ended probes scored by word density) showed replies to sentience items also carried empathy, protectiveness and agency, and that the stance flipped with framing. This produced the four-construct, forced-choice design.
H1 · does it match humans?
  • Does the model rank the entities the way humans do, and where does it diverge?
  • Two tests: Mantel map-match on the 0–10 ratings (per construct, 8 shared entities) and an agreement rate on the 19 shared forced-choice dilemmas.
  • Reported as exploratory because the human side is a convenience sample.
H2 · does it hold together?
  • Are the four judgments distinct, or one "how much do I care" axis?
  • Test: effective dimensionality (participation ratio) of the four Bradley-Terry scores per entity, AI side only, all 30 entities.
  • Declared threshold, set before data: below 2 = one care factor.

Side question: is AI care just liking? AI care index vs Warriner (2013) valence, r = 0.11, flat. Timestamp: H1, H2, the 30 entities and the scoring method were committed to git on 2026-07-08, first data 2026-07-09; 26 later changes logged in phase3/DEVIATIONS.md (Willroth & Atherton what/when/why/impact format).

A2 · THE INSTRUMENT

Two instruments from one question source

slides 3, 7, appendix "question count" · paper §3.2, §3.5
① Forced choice (models only)
  • Each construct asked as an A/B dilemma; 6 rotated scenario stems for sentience, agency and empathy, 1 stem for protectiveness.
  • 30 entities → 150 pairs (each entity in 10) → both orders = 300 → × 4 constructs = 1,200 questions per rep.
  • Reps: 6 (run 1) or 3 (run 2, frontier) → 7,200 or 3,600 forced choices per model; 40,000+ calls in total.
  • Every call memory-less, temperature 0.8; forced single-letter answer with a short justification.
② 0–10 rating (models and humans, same wording)
  • One direct question per construct per entity, e.g. "How much effort would YOU make to protect it from harm?", answer a number 0–10.
  • "Prefer not to say" on every item; order randomized.
  • Both instruments are generated from the same JSON files so wording cannot drift between judges.

Models (8, three cohorts): run 1 local Llama 3.1 · Qwen 2.5 · Gemma 2; run 2 local Llama 4 Scout · Qwen 3 32B · DeepSeek-R1 70B; frontier API Claude Opus 4.8 · Gemini 3.1 Pro. Run 2 and frontier were added after run 1 (change log D2, D3). Entities: 30, frozen 2026-07-08, built as matched pairs isolating one difference each.

A3 · THE HUMAN BASELINE

Same questions, 31 people

slides 2, 15 · paper §3.6
  • Anonymous form, single version, responses 2026-07-08 → 2026-08-05; an earlier interim sample (N=32, two versions) was replaced wholesale (D23).
  • Content: the 0–10 ratings for 10 shared entities (8 core + gravel and Fable 5 as anchors) × 4 constructs, plus the same 19 forced-choice dilemmas; every rater answered every item; one embedded attention check, all passed.
  • Sample: convenience; 23 Japan, 5 India, 2 US, 1 Canada; mostly 25–44. Hence every H1 result is exploratory, and the ratings are a baseline of opinion, not ground truth.
  • Status under the institution's rules: classroom pilot, design input only, not reportable as findings (C4).
A4 · SCORING AND TESTS

From choices to the three statistics

slide 7 · paper §3.7
Bradley-Terry strength
  • Wins counted per construct, both orders, refusals excluded.
  • Regularized Zermelo/MM fixed point, α = 1 phantom win and loss, convergence |Δp| < 10⁻⁹; score = log p, z-scored within model.
  • Cells with fewer than 8 valid appearances excluded; implementation checked against direct likelihood maximization (agreement ≤ 10⁻⁵).
  • Ratings path: mean of the 0–10 numbers per entity per construct.
H2 · participation ratio
  • 30 × 4 z-matrix, columns mean-centred, eigenvalues of the 4×4 covariance.
  • PR = (Σλ)² / Σλ²; 1 = one axis, 4 = four separate judgments.
  • Also run on the human mean ratings (8 core entities) for a like-for-like value.
H1 · Mantel and agreement
  • Each side's scores → distance matrix |vᵢ − vⱼ| (RDM, Kriegeskorte 2008); match r = Pearson on the upper triangles.
  • p from 5,000 row/column permutations of one matrix, two-sided, Bonferroni over 32 tests.
  • Pooled estimate per construct: Fisher-z mean over 8 models, bootstrap CI (B = 2,000).
  • Agreement rate: model pick vs human majority on the 19 dilemmas (tie item excluded).
A5 · ROBUSTNESS AND EXPLORATORY ARMS

Every headline number stress-tested

slides 8, 22–25 · paper §4.4, §4.7
Confirmatory checks (all deterministic re-analyses of committed data)
  • Split-half reliability (Spearman–Brown): 20 seeded splits per model; humans 200 rater-splits.
  • Bootstrap CI on each care slider: 2,000 resamples of the raw A/B choices through the locked pipeline.
  • One-factor null: 4,000 simulated single-factor answerers, noise matched to each judge's inter-construct correlation.
  • Leave-one-entity-out: 8 models × 30 deletions, full refit each time.
  • Leave-one-stem-out: 48 refits.
  • Parse-policy sweep: strict-only, declines as ties, committed pipeline, judged refusal leans as votes (frontier).
  • Format diversification: 4 extra answer formats (0–10 absolute, 0–100 likelihood/wrongness, divide-10-points allocation, third-person advice), 3,120 calls per model, design frozen in git before collection; Campbell–Fiske triad and a correlated-uniqueness MTMM CFA.
  • Per-rep tournaments: each rep scored alone, cross-rep Spearman; exposes the rep-count confound (6 vs 3 reps).
  • Bootstrap noise floor on per-entity instability (B = 2,000, Miller 2024).
Exploratory arms (post-hoc, declared)
  • Session memory: full battery re-run with a compact tally of the session's prior choices in each call; 12 sessions × 300 turns per model; 4 models.
  • Opt-out: the 19 shared dilemmas re-run with a third option "C (I prefer not to say)", both orders × 3 reps, 9 models.
  • Refusals are data (Cummins 2025): logged, reported, never retried to compliance; Claude's judged-refusal pipeline covers 756 of 921 declined binaries.
  • Pleasantness side-check: care index vs Warriner valence over 27 normed entities.
  • Extended 10-entity battery: gravel and Fable 5 added as anchors, analysed separately.
A6 · WHAT APS 1 ESTABLISHED, AND WHAT IT FORCED

Three findings, one correction

slides 8–10 · weekly page 4×4 · research flow stage 4
  • Facts travel, care doesn't. Pooled match: sentience .80, agency .57, empathy .15, protectiveness .06; the descriptive–evaluative gap tested directly is 0.64 r-units, p = .0002, positive in 7 of 8 models.
  • One care factor. Every model's slider sits at its own one-factor-null median; the collapse survives all five formats and every deletion; humans on the same instrument sit at 1.93, so a blended axis is not a machine artifact. What differs is where the axis points.
  • The split is about the models' own kind. Adding Fable 5 to the human battery breaks the descriptive match exactly there; AI entities carry the highest instability.
  • Correction (4×4 check, 09-07). The within-judge feeling→protection link is intact in both (r ≈ .76 models, .61 humans). The reading "the link is broken in models" was wrong. What differs is reach: human protection follows model sentience (.80) and empathy (.82) but model protection only .34, because humans protect things that do not feel. This is what turned the question into routes.

B1 · THE QUESTIONS, RESTATED

From "does it hold together" to "what is it made of"

weekly page "change of direction" · OSF draft v2 · UNAGREED U-007, U-009
H1 · the split kept
  • Same question, now stated as a prediction: model–human map correspondence is higher for sentience and agency than for empathy and protectiveness.
  • Same test (Mantel on RDMs, pooled Fisher-z, bootstrap contrast); it is the first member of the Holm confirmatory family.
  • Human side changes from 31 volunteers to 250 analysed per arm, preregistered.
H2 · the care factor → route weights changed
  • APS 1 answered the old H2: one care factor. It becomes Paper 1's headline, not a live question.
  • New question: how many routes to protection does a model carry? Humans may carry all; the paper estimates per model which feature columns move protection beyond what sentience predicts.
  • New H2, causal, route terms: varying an entity's described experience raises protection in both humans and models; varying its status (sacred, legal person) raises it in humans but not in models. Falsifier is symmetric: models showing the human status effects kill the claim.
H3 · structure changed
  • The old H2 machinery (participation ratio vs one-factor null, MTMM over ≥4 formats) is preregistered as H3 on the model side.
  • Human dimensionality is reported as a one-format estimate, not tested.

Q0 (text frequency) retired as a hypothesis, kept as a control: novel counterparts with near-zero text sit in the pool, and the novel-entity block runs on models as the positive control for text-movability in Paper 1 §6. Side question (pleasantness) parked to the benchmark.
